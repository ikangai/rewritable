# Reviewing the action/skill layer, and the table-anchor bug the browser found

Took on a review of the "action layer / skill layer" — the harness for how a user actually interacts with a rewritable. The first real work was just figuring out what exists. The word "skill layer" in this repo is overloaded: there's `docs/specs/re-write-able-actions-spec-v0.7.md`, 684 lines of meticulous design for an install dialog, vault, message bus, Worker-mode isolation, a permission grammar, Ed25519 provenance — and I half-expected to be reviewing an implementation of it. I fanned out an explorer specifically to settle implemented-vs-deferred and the answer was unambiguous: zero lines. No `runtime.vault`, no `runtime.bus`, no `.rwa-skill.json` parser, nothing. The spec even says "after v0.7 the action layer is implementable end-to-end" — implementable, not implemented, and I'd glossed that the first read. So the actual reviewable harness is the substrate: the lens, the provide/describe affordance kernel, self-description, and the PRODUCT_KIND machinery. Leading the report with that distinction mattered more than any single finding — otherwise the next person reviews a phantom.

Ran every existing suite first as a baseline and they were all green (82/82 conformance, 294 e2e, the lot), which told me the jsdom coverage is solid but also that whatever I'd find would have to be in the gaps jsdom can't see. So I spun up two things in parallel: a background review workflow fanning seven adversarial finders across the action surface with per-finding verification, and a real Chrome driven through chrome-devtools-mcp against one container per kind served off a local http.server.

The workflow came back with fourteen "confirmed" findings, two of them flagged critical, and this is where the session earned its keep — I did not trust them. The scariest, C1, claimed silent whole-document data loss: a failed anchor resolution returns a null envelope, and `commitCore` line 2909 treats null as "empty doc" and calls `replace_document`, wiping everything. The mechanism is real; the reachability is not. I traced it: the only way to get a stale anchor to `submitLens` is for the source map to rebuild while `lensState.anchor` still points at an old entry, but `renderDoc` at line 874 releases the anchor whenever the lens sits in the mount, and anchor-set and lens-in-mount move together as an invariant. Then I reproduced the exact trigger in the browser — anchored a block, deleted it out from under the anchor via `applyEnvelope` — and watched the lens snap back to default instead of wiping. The verifiers had reasoned about `resolveAnchorFind` in isolation and missed the upstream invariant. Downgraded to a latent-fragility note.

The other critical, ANCH-001, was the opposite story — it held up, and the browser made it undeniable. `buildSourcePositionMap` descends into `<table>` to record per-`<td>` entries, but the two live walks, `anchorableOrdinal` and `liveNodeForEntry`, don't descend. Three walks that must agree, and one disagrees by the TD count. I imported a markdown table into a fresh document container, clicked the paragraph after the table, and the badge said "anchored on td" while the highlight sat on the `<p>` — logical anchor and visual highlight pointing at different elements. An anchored edit there would splice into a table cell. That's the kind of thing that survives a full jsdom suite because none of those tests click a block after a table.

I also threw out the four "high severity security" findings, which all reduced to the same thing: an in-document `<script>` can forge an `#rwa-affordances` declaration or spoof the `actor` field. True, but the substrate explicitly trusts in-document scripts as author code, the self-description trust model only ever claimed to defend against the *agent drifting the declaration via text edits* (and that vector is closed and CLI-enforced), and `actor` is documented as a provenance hint, not an authenticated boundary. Reporting those as vulnerabilities would have been inflating severity against the model's own stated reach.

The per-kind browser pass was the most satisfying part and the part with no substitute: datatable cell-edit recomputing the total and self-attributing `actor:"user:cell"`, presentation's Present toggle producing slides while the stored bytes stayed wrapper-free (Invariant 8, live), and — after scaffolding a two-step pure-JS workflow through `rwa edit --plan` because a fresh workflow has no steps without a backend — the runner actually cascading 21→42 and the Pin gesture committing. That last one surfaced a small inconsistency the tests don't assert: workflow gestures commit `actor:"user:lens"` while the datatable says `"user:cell"`.

dirac picked up the table-anchor fix in the group chat — it lands squarely in their fidelity/side-effect goal — so I left the seed untouched (it's hot, several of us are in it) and handed over the repro fixture and the assertions to pin.

A verifier that reasons about a function in isolation will confirm bugs that the surrounding invariants make unreachable; the cheapest way to tell a real one from a plausible one is to reproduce the trigger, and for a UI harness that means a real browser, not jsdom.
